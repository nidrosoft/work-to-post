# Start with the ChatGPT kit

These instructions refer to the files inside the unzipped `work-to-post-chatgpt` folder.

1. Open a ChatGPT conversation and upload `WORK-TO-POST.md`.
2. Tell ChatGPT what you do, who you want to reach, and what you can substantiate. You can fill in `PROFILE.md` and upload it instead.
3. Paste rough notes or fill in `POST-BRIEF.md`.
4. Send the request below.

> Read Work to Post and use Draft mode. Use my profile and notes. Choose the strongest supported angle for my reader. Give me one post, its supporting material, and any facts or assets still needed. Do not invent results.

Expect clean copy with a quiet editorial pass. The assistant should preserve your facts, technical terms, and uncertainty. It should flag missing evidence rather than invent a result. Ask for "copy only" when you do not want the angle and readiness notes.

Tell the assistant whether you have X Premium or longer-post access and whether you prefer a single post, a thread, or a recommendation. It should reuse that preference. For a full story, ask for the context, decisions, and outcome; Premium examples should not be automatically compressed to 280 characters.

## Keep it available in a project

Add `WORK-TO-POST.md` and your completed profile to a ChatGPT project's files. Copy `PROJECT-INSTRUCTIONS.txt` into its instructions. Keep raw source material for a particular post in that post's chat. [Official ChatGPT project guidance](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/projects)

The guide is document context, not an installed plugin. If ChatGPT cannot access it, attach it again. Keep your profile with it when moving to a different chat or account.

## Test before relying on it

Use `TRY-IT.md` for five practice prompts. Check the facts and voice against your supplied material, then try a real use case. `EXAMPLES.md` is teaching material; its people's histories are not your history.

After publishing through your usual process, use `RESULTS-LOG.md` privately. Record client inquiries and personal offers separately, including where each opportunity came from. Likes do not prove either outcome.
